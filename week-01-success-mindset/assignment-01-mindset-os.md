## Task 3 — What Does Your 2.0 Version Look Like?

Write and publicly publish an article about your future professional self, written as if a journalist is writing about you **3–7 years from now**.

Your article must:

* Be at least **300 words**.
* Be written in the **past tense**, as if it has already happened.
* Include specific proof such as projects, portfolio, GitHub, blogs, certifications, job role, leadership, or community contribution.
* Be published on LinkedIn, Medium, WordPress, Blogspot, a personal blog, or a portfolio page.

### My Article

# From Frontend Developer to DevOps Engineer: The Journey of Building a Career Through Consistency

**By 2031**

In 2031, Ayobami Lawal became an example of what consistent learning, determination, and practical experience could achieve in the technology industry.

His journey did not begin with years of cloud engineering experience or advanced technical knowledge. He started with a background in Computer Science and frontend development, but he wanted to explore a different side of technology.

After completing his university education and gaining experience in IT systems and infrastructure support during his NYSC, he developed a stronger interest in cloud computing, automation, and infrastructure management.

Rather than waiting for the perfect opportunity, he began learning DevOps from the basics. He studied Linux, networking, Git, AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, and CI/CD pipelines. The journey was not always easy. He encountered errors, struggled with unfamiliar concepts, and spent time troubleshooting problems. However, he continued practising and improving his skills.

One of his major achievements was building a practical DevOps portfolio that demonstrated his ability to work with real-world technologies. His GitHub profile featured projects involving Docker containerization, Kubernetes deployments, infrastructure automation with Terraform, and CI/CD pipelines using GitHub Actions and Jenkins.

He also documented his learning experiences through technical articles, project walkthroughs, and tutorials. His LinkedIn presence grew as he shared lessons from his projects, explained technical concepts, and connected with other professionals in the cloud and DevOps community.

His dedication eventually opened doors to a professional opportunity as a DevOps Engineer. In that role, he worked with development teams to automate deployments, manage cloud infrastructure, improve application reliability, and streamline software delivery.

Beyond his professional responsibilities, Ayobami contributed to the technology community by sharing knowledge with beginners who were trying to enter the DevOps and cloud engineering space. He encouraged others to focus on practical experience instead of simply collecting certificates.

Looking back, his biggest achievement was not just securing a job or mastering different technologies. It was the ability to remain consistent even when results were not immediate.

His story demonstrated that career transitions were possible, that beginners could become skilled professionals, and that small improvements made consistently could lead to significant achievements.

**His journey started with a decision to learn, but it grew through action, patience, and consistency.**
